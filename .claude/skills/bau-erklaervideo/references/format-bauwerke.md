# Format C — „Bauwerke“ (famous bridges, towers, buildings, tunnels)

The series' third format (user's idea, 2026-11-20: „noch größere Sachen
… berühmte Brücken oder Bauwerke, allgemein Architektur“). It explains
**how a famous structure stands, holds or works** — the trick that keeps
the Burj Khalifa upright in the wind, why the Leaning Tower of Pisa
doesn't fall, how the Golden Gate Bridge hangs on two cables. The
It is type C of the nine types picked at random (`themenarten.md`, SKILL.md step 0).

Everything else is the house standard: Jan, the realistic look, captions,
keywords, name tags, the first-3-seconds rules, the hook that leaves a
question in the head, one core idea, payoff, "Übrigens", the CTA at the
end, cover, descriptions, learning log. This file only lists what is
**different** from `script-formel.md` and `visual-style.md`.

## Why it works

- **Sweet spot by nature:** a structure with its own famous name that
  millions know from photos or have stood on (Eiffelturm, Golden Gate,
  Stephansdom, Gotthard-Basistunnel) — not „Brücken allgemein“, not an
  unknown viaduct.
- **Scale is the spectacle:** the picture can show things no photo can —
  the whole bridge in x-ray, the wind pushing the tower, the foundation
  deep in the ground, a person tiny next to a single cable.
- **A felt moment is built in:** standing at the top, the glass floor,
  the tower swaying, the bridge vibrating under a truck — the viewer
  feels the height.

## Topic rules

- **Famous by name and seen by millions** (AT/CH/DE first: Stephansdom,
  Prater-Riesenrad, Europabrücke, Gotthard-Basistunnel, Kapellbrücke,
  Kölner Dom, Berliner Fernsehturm, Elbphilharmonie; world icons:
  Eiffelturm, Golden Gate, Burj Khalifa, Pisa, Kolosseum, Pyramiden,
  Taipei 101, Hoover Dam, Sydney Opera).
- **One trick, one core idea** („Der Trick ist: \_\_\_“): the damper ball in
  Taipei 101, the leaning tower's soft ground, the arch pushing outward,
  the cables carrying the deck, the dome's ring of iron. Not the history
  of the building, not a list of records.
- **A „Was?!“ number with a comparison** (max 2 numbers): „Der Eiffelturm
  ist im Sommer 15 cm größer.“ — always checked (web search).
- **Du-Bezug:** the viewer is there — „Du stehst ganz oben im Burj
  Khalifa …“, „Du fährst über die Europabrücke …“. Safety rules of
  format B apply to any risky act (warnings only).

## Script shape (exactly 45 s, 130–142 words — as format A)

| Part                    | When       | Job                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Hook** (line 1)       | 0–3 s      | the structure + a claim with a hidden reason („Darum fällt der Turm von Pisa nicht um.“)               |
| **Beat 1**              | 3–13 s     | you are there, the felt moment (height, wind, the lean); second money shot ~10 s                       |
| **Beat 2**              | 13–21 s    | the problem the builders had (wind, soft ground, weight, water)                                        |
| **Mid twist**           | ~21–25 s   | „Rate mal“ or the surprise (it nearly fell, it moves, it's hollow …)                                   |
| **Beat 3**              | 25–33 s    | the trick, shown in x-ray — max 2 terms, one everyday comparison                                       |
| **Übrigens** (optional) | 31–35 s    | one strange fact that makes the question bigger (before the answer)                                    |
| **Payoff**              | 35–40 s    | the answer **as a picture**, fast reveal — the last content                                            |
| **CTA** (last line)     | ~39–44.3 s | comment question about the viewer's own experience („Warst du schon oben?“) + „Folge für mehr Wissen.“ |

## Picture

- **Frame 0:** the structure big and instantly recognisable (its famous
  silhouette, the angle of the postcard) **plus** motion — the lean, the
  sway, a gust, a train rolling over, the elevator shooting up.
- **Scale:** always one human or the viewer's hand in the shot to read
  the size; one giant-scale comparison as a money shot (a whale in the
  cable, a bus in the damper ball, the Stephansdom next to the Burj).
- **The world is the real place** (SKILL.md 1b): the Seine with Paris
  roofs, the bay with fog under the Golden Gate, the Alps around the
  Europabrücke — still the realistic finish up close, low-poly far away.
- **X-ray / cut-away** shows the hidden structure: foundation, cables,
  steel core, the damper — only the 1–3 parts of the trick.
- **Forces as arrows** (one kind at a time): wind, weight, tension.
- The **cover** shows the structure at its most dramatic moment (the
  lean exaggerated, the sway, the storm) with a question title
  („Warum <em>kippt</em> er nicht?“).
